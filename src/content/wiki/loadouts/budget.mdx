A budget loadout is not the cheapest possible collection of items. It is the **lowest-cost kit that can still perform the intended job reliably** while leaving enough persistent cash for another attempt.

## Budget order of operations

| Step | Buy for | Stop when |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | The range and task you actually expect | The weapon already covers the mission |
| Ammo | Enough for the intended life | Extra supply no longer improves survival time |
| Storage | The gear you truly need to carry | Empty capacity becomes paid weight |
| Medical | Survive ordinary damage and support | Additional items become unlikely to be used |
| Protection | Reduce realistic risk | Cost and weight begin to hurt the role plan |

## Cheap does not mean incomplete

A low-cost build that cannot carry ammunition, cannot heal after the first contact or cannot perform the chosen support role is not a budget build; it is simply under-equipped. Budgeting is about eliminating **unnecessary spend**, not removing the core function.

## Why replacement cost matters

Cash persists across matches, so learning with a sustainable kit creates more attempts. This is especially useful while learning map routes, aircraft controls, building workflows or a new weapon class. Repeated useful lives can be more valuable than one heavily upgraded life followed by several broke ones.

## When to upgrade

Upgrade when you can name the specific problem the purchase solves. More range, more protection, a role tool or better storage can be worth the cost when the next task requires it. Avoid upgrading only because a higher tier is unlocked.

## Budget checklist

- Can I afford to lose this kit more than once?
- Does every purchased item support the task?
- Do I have enough storage for ammo and utility?
- Is protection appropriate for the expected fight?
- Am I saving money for a vehicle or support tool this life actually needs?
