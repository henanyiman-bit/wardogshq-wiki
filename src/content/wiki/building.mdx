# WARDOGS Building Wiki

Building is a core battlefield system in WARDOGS that connects construction, logistics and team support. This hub explains how the building system works, how structures are used, and how players can combine construction with battlefield objectives.

## Building System Overview

The building system allows players to create useful battlefield positions instead of relying only on existing locations. Effective construction is about choosing the right location, managing supplies and creating value for teammates.

Construction should begin with a battlefield problem rather than an empty patch of ground. A team may need a protected approach, a place to regroup, a safer logistics stop or a position that supports pressure near the active Control Zone. The useful build is the one that solves that problem without consuming more time and resources than the position can return.

Building also changes the rhythm of a match. Infantry can turn temporary control into a position that is easier to revisit, while logistics players create the supply flow that keeps construction relevant. Because objectives and routes change, a structure should be evaluated as part of the current match state, not as a permanent answer for every round.

## Construction Mechanics

The practical construction loop has four parts: choose a purpose, confirm access, commit resources and maintain the result. Location matters before placement. Builders should check how teammates will reach the site, where supplies can arrive, which direction threats are likely to approach from and whether the active objective is close enough for the position to matter.

Once work starts, the builder should avoid blocking friendly movement or creating a position that can only be entered from one predictable direction. Structures work best when they shape movement while leaving teammates clear routes to deploy, resupply and leave. A technically strong wall can still be a tactical mistake if it traps vehicles, exposes a narrow doorway or separates defenders from the objective.

Construction is also a team commitment. The player placing objects spends time that could otherwise be used for combat, transport or scouting. Teammates gain value only when they understand what the position is for. Simple communication—where the entrance is, what the base supports and which route needs protection—makes a small build more useful than a large but confusing layout.

## Structure Types

Different structures serve different purposes. Defensive structures help protect important areas, while support structures improve the ability of a team to maintain pressure during a match.

Use cover and barriers to break exposed sightlines, guide movement and give defenders a place to recover. These pieces are most effective when they protect a real task such as holding an approach, unloading supplies or preparing a push. Avoid placing cover only because space is available; every object should improve safety, access or battlefield awareness.

Support-oriented construction includes the positions and infrastructure that help a team return to useful activity. A forward location can shorten travel, concentrate supplies or connect players to nearby systems. Its value comes from repeated use. If the team cannot reach it, supply it or safely operate from it, the structure is functioning as scenery rather than support.

## Resource Requirements

Building is connected to the wider WARDOGS economy and logistics layer. Before committing to a site, confirm that the required purchase and supply plan fit the team's current priorities. A costly construction decision can compete with weapons, vehicles and other tools funded from persistent cash, so the expected team benefit should justify the investment.

Resources are not only the material used to place a structure. Travel time, vehicle access, builder attention and defensive manpower are part of the real cost. A position far from a reliable route may require repeated transport runs. A position that attracts constant pressure may need defenders who could otherwise contest the objective. Good planning accounts for those continuing demands before the first structure is placed.

## Defensive Building

Defensive construction should delay, redirect or expose attackers without making friendly movement equally difficult. Protect the side most likely to receive pressure, but preserve more than one way for teammates to enter and leave. Consider nearby elevation, long sightlines and vehicle routes; a wall facing the obvious road does little if an attacker can overlook it from adjacent terrain.

Layered defense is more useful than a single hard edge. An outer observation point can provide warning, a protected working area can keep support players active, and a fallback position can prevent one breach from ending the entire defense. Do not assume the structure will defend itself. Players still need visibility, communication and a plan for threats that bypass the main approach.

## Building Strategy

Start by reading the Control Zone and the routes connecting it to friendly movement. A build close to the action is not automatically useful: it needs enough separation to remain usable while still shortening reinforcement, supply or staging time. Reassess the site when the objective shifts. Continuing to invest in a position that no longer supports the match can turn a good opening decision into wasted resources.

Coordinate construction with vehicle and infantry plans. Logistics vehicles need an approach that is not blocked by defensive pieces. Infantry need exits that do not force everyone into the same line of fire. Builders should also avoid advertising the entire team's route with an isolated structure that can be seen long before defenders are ready.

The best strategy is usually incremental. Establish the minimum useful position, watch how teammates and enemies interact with it, and add structures only when a clear need appears. This keeps the layout readable and limits the cost of a site that proves unsafe or irrelevant.

## Beginner Tips

- Build near objectives that provide strategic value.
- Consider supply access before placing structures.
- Coordinate construction with your squad instead of building isolated positions.
- Protect important structures after investing resources.
- Leave clear vehicle lanes and more than one infantry exit.
- Build the smallest useful position before expanding it.
- Check nearby high ground and long sightlines before committing resources.
- Tell teammates what the position supports and where supplies should arrive.
- Stop investing when the active objective or team route moves elsewhere.

New builders should follow experienced support players for a few lives and observe which positions receive repeated use. That reveals more than copying a layout without understanding its purpose. After each build, ask whether teammates reached the fight faster, survived a dangerous approach or maintained supplies more reliably. If the answer is no, change the location or simplify the plan next time.
