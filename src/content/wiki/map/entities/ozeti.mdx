## Terrain and orientation

Hill-town terrain with a central settlement, winding roads and a sports field landmark. The central settlement and the rectangular sports field to its south are the strongest orientation landmarks. Roads fan out through the hills, so route choice matters before a squad commits to the town.

The safest way to use a fixed terrain image is to match **two or more visible landmarks** before making a route decision. A road junction plus a building cluster is more reliable than assuming one visual feature uniquely identifies your position.

## Control Zone and support systems

The map is only the base layer. WARDOGS adds the active **Control Zone**, higher-value **Hot Zone** behavior, **Towers**, **Drills**, **FOBs** and vehicle routes on top of it. Those systems explain why a useful map Wiki needs more than a screenshot: players need to know which part of the terrain matters **this match**.

## Practical map checklist

1. Confirm the map name before using coordinates.
2. Locate the current Control Zone.
3. Match at least two terrain landmarks.
4. Check whether nearby towers or support systems are relevant to the active objective.
5. Choose infantry, ground transport or air transport based on distance and route.
6. Re-check live terrain and markers if the community reference disagrees with the game.
