WARDOGS players use “Towers” to describe a map-control mechanic currently associated with **code fragments** and the **Hot Zone**. Same-game player reports describe capturing Towers, collecting pieces of a code and then using a completed code to influence where the Hot Zone moves.

> **Evidence boundary:** the Tower-code workflow is supported primarily by current WARDOGS community observations rather than a complete developer-authored mechanics document. Treat exact capture steps, timing and code behavior as Early Access information that can change.

## What Towers are for

The practical reason players care about Towers is not the structure itself; it is the reported ability to affect a high-value map system. Because the Hot Zone can reward double cash, any mechanic that influences where that zone appears can affect travel, conflict density, economic opportunity and team planning.

That is why Tower questions are usually strategic questions: “What does this Tower do?”, “How do I get the code?”, or “Why is my team capturing these instead of moving directly to the current fight?”

## The reported code-fragment loop

Current player explanations generally describe the loop in four parts:

1. A team captures or interacts with Towers.
2. Tower activity reveals or awards pieces of a code.
3. The team assembles the full code.
4. The completed code can be used at a Tower to influence the Hot Zone.

This Wiki intentionally does not publish a fixed number of fragments, exact timing window or guaranteed interaction sequence because those values are not established strongly enough in the official source set.

## Why Towers matter strategically

If a team can influence Hot Zone placement, Tower control can become a way to shape where high-value fighting occurs. That can favor a team that already has transport, a forward position or a supply route ready near the desired area.

The mechanic also creates a tradeoff. Time spent capturing Towers is time not spent directly contesting the current Control Zone. A Tower play is therefore most useful when the team understands what it is trying to gain from the future Hot Zone position.

## Towers and the economy

The Hot Zone is described by Team17 as a double-cash opportunity. That means Tower strategy can indirectly become economy strategy. Pulling high-value activity toward a location your team can reach or support more easily can create better earning conditions, but only if the team can actually capitalize on the move.

The [Money guide](/money/) explains why persistent cash makes those decisions matter beyond a single engagement.

## Towers versus Drills

Towers and Drills are often discussed together because current player reports connect both systems to Hot Zone influence, but they are not the same workflow. Towers are associated with the code-fragment path. [Drills](/map/drills/) are associated with forward-base construction, supplies and a Drill Rig.

Keeping the two pages separate avoids mixing community observations into one vague “map mechanic” article.

## How to approach Tower information during Early Access

When a patch changes map systems, verify the current in-game interaction before relying on an old guide. WARDOGS launched into Early Access with active balance and feature iteration, and even officially documented progression values have already moved between levels. Tower behavior deserves the same caution.

Use this page as a conceptual explanation of why Towers matter, not as a permanent frame-perfect interaction manual.
