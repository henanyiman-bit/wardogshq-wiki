## Weapon classes explained

### Assault rifles
Assault rifles are the broadest general-purpose category in the current dataset. They cover mid-range fighting and make sense for players who do not yet know whether a route will become a close objective push or a longer sightline. The table shows substantial price and damage differences, so “assault rifle” is not a single economic choice.

### SMGs
SMGs emphasize close-range mobility and high rates of fire. Their shorter effective ranges make them easier to justify when the plan is building interiors, dense objective fights or vehicle crews that expect to dismount close to the action.

### LMGs
LMGs are heavier and oriented toward sustained fire. Their weight and price mean they are better understood as support investments than as automatic upgrades over lighter rifles.

### Marksman and sniper rifles
Marksman rifles preserve follow-up speed while stretching engagement distance; dedicated sniper rifles trade even more toward precision and range. Because WARDOGS maps are huge but the active Control Zone concentrates players, long-range equipment is useful only if the player's position still contributes to the objective.

### Launchers
Launchers support anti-vehicle, anti-fortification and specialist tasks. The launcher vendor screenshot above also shows that progression gates are a real part of the equipment system.

## Ammo and attachment notes
Official Season 1 material explicitly names **7.62x54mm AP**, **5.56mm AP** and the **PP-19 50-round drum magazine** as progression items. That proves ammunition and attachments are progression-relevant, but it does not provide a complete public penetration or compatibility database. Individual weapon pages therefore avoid inventing attachment trees that are not verified.

## How to use this database
1. Start with the weapon class that matches the task.
2. Compare price against your current bankroll, not against an abstract “best gun” list.
3. Use range and RPM as context for expected fights.
4. Treat missing fields as unavailable data, not zero.
5. Re-check live values after patches.
