WARDOGS is a large-scale, three-team tactical shooter where objective play, money, equipment, transport and construction all connect. New players do not need to master every system at once. Start by understanding the active objective, use an affordable loadout and stay close enough to teammates to contribute.

## Match Basics

Each match brings three teams into the same battlefield. Your immediate goal is not simply to collect eliminations: it is to help your team reach, contest and hold the active Control Zone. Check the map before moving, identify a sensible route and avoid crossing long distances alone when transport or a nearby squad is available.

Useful actions include fighting near the objective, reviving teammates, transporting players and supplies, and supporting forward positions. Choose one clear job for each life. A modest contribution performed consistently is more valuable than wandering away from the fight with expensive equipment.

## Control Zone Explained

The Control Zone is the central scoring area. The team with the strongest player presence in the active zone earns points, and the first team to reach 100 points wins. Because the zone can draw combat into different parts of the wider battlefield, always confirm its location after spawning and when the flow of the match changes.

Approach the zone with cover, teammates and a plan. Holding nearby routes, protecting revives and disrupting enemy reinforcements can be as important as standing in the open center. For the detailed scoring loop, read the [Control Zone guide](/map/control-zone/).

## Starting Cash

Players begin with $10,000. Cash pays for weapons, equipment and vehicles, and it persists between matches. Treat every purchase as a decision about risk: an expensive kit may be powerful, but losing it before contributing can leave fewer options for later lives.

Money can be earned through useful battlefield actions, including objective participation, transport, revives and logistics. The [Money and Economy guide](/money/) explains the broader system and the [making money guide](/money/how-to-make-money/) compares practical income routes.

## First Loadout

For an early match, choose a dependable primary weapon, enough ammunition for the role and only the utility you understand how to use. Avoid filling every slot simply because it is available. A lighter, affordable kit makes it easier to learn positioning and objective timing without placing too much pressure on a single life.

Build around your intended task. Infantry near the Control Zone needs reliable combat equipment. A medic should preserve space for revive-focused tools. Drivers and logistics players should prioritize the equipment needed to complete transport or supply work. Use the [Loadouts hub](/loadouts/) when you are ready to compare role-based options.

## Beginner Strategy

1. Check the Control Zone before leaving the spawn area.
2. Buy a loadout you can afford to lose.
3. Move with teammates or use transport instead of running alone.
4. Fight close enough to the objective for your actions to matter.
5. Revive, transport or resupply when direct combat is not the best contribution.
6. Preserve cash by disengaging from fights that cannot help the team.
7. Review what worked before making a more expensive purchase.

The battlefield is large, so timing matters. Arriving together is usually stronger than entering the same fight one player at a time. Watch where teammates are spawning, which routes vehicles are using and where forward positions are being established.

## Common Mistakes

- Ignoring the Control Zone while chasing distant fights.
- Spending most starting cash on an unfamiliar loadout or vehicle.
- Crossing the map alone when teammates or transport are available.
- Treating every life as a pure elimination run instead of supporting the objective.
- Taking a vehicle without a clear battlefield purpose.
- Building or moving supplies without checking what the frontline needs.
- Repeating the same approach after the objective or enemy pressure changes.
