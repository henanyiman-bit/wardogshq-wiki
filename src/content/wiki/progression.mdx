## How progression actually works

<div class="progression-flow">
  <div><span>01</span><strong>Play a role</strong><small>Combat, revive, support, driving or flying activity contributes to progression.</small></div>
  <div><span>02</span><strong>Earn XP</strong><small>Season 1 changed the XP curve from level 6 upward.</small></div>
  <div><span>03</span><strong>Reach an unlock gate</strong><small>Items and vehicles can move between levels and tracks after balance patches.</small></div>
  <div><span>04</span><strong>Pay for access</strong><small>Some unlocks have their own permanent purchase cost.</small></div>
  <div><span>05</span><strong>Buy it for a life</strong><small>Your persistent cash still controls whether the newly available item is affordable now.</small></div>
</div>

> **Season 1 snapshot:** BULKHEAD explicitly said the beta progression curve was designed for short test weekends and changed it for Early Access. Treat old beta progression charts as historical unless the live client still matches them.

  <strong>About “Assault” terminology</strong>
  <p>Current community progression guides also describe an Assault role line. The official Season 1 changelog used the labels Career, Recon, Medic, Support, Driver and Pilot in its economy/unlock sections. Because interface naming can change by build, this page keeps the official Season 1 labels as the primary reference and treats additional role labels as client-dependent unless directly shown in the current game.</p>

## Track-by-track reference

### Career
Career is the broadest label in the Season 1 unlock list. It includes general equipment such as the Sports Parachute and Large Backpack, ammunition upgrades, the Deagle and the late Artillery Tank gate. The **Artillery Tank moving from level 55 to 90** is the clearest reminder that a “known unlock level” can change drastically between testing and Early Access.

### Medic
The explicit Season 1 Medic unlock move was the **PP-19 50-round drum magazine from level 33 to 29**. Medic progression matters because WARDOGS directly rewards revive-oriented teamwork, but an unlock page should not turn that into an unsupported claim that every Medic action awards a fixed XP amount.

### Pilot
The launch notes do not list Pilot level moves in the same way as Driver, but they do list Pilot-linked economy changes: the small armoured supply crate, Little Bird with miniguns and **Z20 Lakota** all received price changes. That is enough to confirm Pilot as a live progression/economy label without inventing a full level ladder.

## Unlocking, buying and using are three different decisions

<div class="progression-cost-grid">
  <div><span>1</span><strong>Unlock gate</strong><p>Reach the required level or track condition.</p></div>
  <div><span>2</span><strong>Permanent access cost</strong><p>Some items can require a one-time unlock payment.</p></div>
  <div><span>3</span><strong>Per-life purchase</strong><p>You still spend persistent cash when adding gear or vehicles to a life.</p></div>
</div>

This distinction is why progression pages should not say “you get a Heavy Tank at Driver 35.” A more accurate reading is: **Driver 35 can gate access, while the economy still determines whether you can afford to unlock and deploy it.**

## Practical progression checklist

1. Identify the track tied to the job you actually enjoy.
2. Use the current client to confirm the item’s live gate before grinding specifically for it.
3. Separate **XP requirement**, **unlock cost** and **per-life price** in your planning.
4. Recheck patch notes before relying on beta-era charts.
5. Use [Money](/money/) and [Loadouts](/loadouts/) to decide whether an unlocked item is economically practical.
6. Use [Vehicles](/vehicles/) or [Weapons](/weapons/) for entity-level stats instead of turning progression level into a performance ranking.
